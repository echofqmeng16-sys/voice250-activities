In-Class Activity ① · about 9 minutes

Beethoven's Two Worlds
— The Brook & The “Ode to Joy”

Two symphonies from the same year (1824): one wanders the countryside, one gathers all of humanity. Two games, one composer.

👤 First things first — your name

Please enter your name (or student ID). The timer starts as soon as you tap “Start”.

🔎 How it works

  1. Game 1 — Symphony No. 6 “Pastorale”, 2nd movement (“Scene by the Brook”): at the very end, three instruments imitate three birds. Tap “I hear a bird!” every time — how many calls can you catch?
  2. Game 2 — Symphony No. 9, finale (“Ode to Joy”): the famous tune arrives quietly and grows like a sunrise. Tap “NEW WAVE!” each time it comes back with MORE forces behind it.
  3. Vote + quiz: which world do you enter more easily — nature, or brotherhood?
  4. Submit: tap the green button at the bottom when you finish.

🐦 Game 1 · Scene by the Brook — bird-call hunt

Listening tip: stay patient through the gentle stream music — near the end, the solo woodwinds start talking like birds.

The three birds of the brook: 🕊️ Nightingale (flute) · 🐤 Quail (oboe) · 🐦 Cuckoo (clarinet). Beethoven tells us exactly which instruments play which bird — the oldest “program notes” in a score!

🌊 Game 2 · “Ode to Joy” — count the waves

Listening tip: in the finale, cellos and basses alone hum the tune first — almost shy. Then it returns again and again, each time bigger. Tap at every wave:

0

waves you rode (most performances: 4–6)

What you just heard: melody → variations → full orchestra → CHORUS + orchestra (the shock of 1824: voices inside a symphony!) → everything at once. Beethoven turns one tune into a sunrise — a crescendo that never needs to get louder, only fuller.
