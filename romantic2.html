One voice, four characters. Schubert changes the singer's pitch and the piano's pattern to switch scenes — low and calm (father), high and gasping (child), soft and sweet (the Erlking — that's what makes it terrifying). The piano never stops galloping… until it does.

🎭 Game 3 · Berlioz — same theme, or twisted?

Listen to Excerpt A (1st movement) and Excerpt B (5th movement, the “Witches’ Sabbath”). Then vote:

It IS the same melody. Berlioz calls it the idée fixe — the “fixed idea”, a melody representing the beloved (an actress he was obsessed with). It stalks all five movements, twisted by each scene: dreamy in the ballroom… grotesque at the witches' sabbath. Liszt borrowed the trick and named it thematic transformation.
